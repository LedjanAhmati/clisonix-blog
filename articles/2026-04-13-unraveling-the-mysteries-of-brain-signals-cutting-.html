As we stand at the cusp of a new era in healthcare, one crucial aspect remains shrouded in mystery: the intricacies of brain signals. The human brain is a vast, complex network of electrical impulses, neural connections, and synapses that work in harmony to enable our thoughts, emotions, and actions. Deciphering these signals holds the key to revolutionizing patient care, from diagnosing neurological disorders to developing personalized treatments.

However, signal processing poses a formidable challenge in healthcare. Brain signals are inherently noisy, and extracting meaningful patterns requires advanced techniques and sophisticated algorithms. The problem is further compounded by the sheer volume of data generated by modern medical equipment, such as EEGs, fMRI scans, and EMG sensors.

**The Problem: Real Challenges in Signal Processing**

Current signal processing methods often rely on traditional techniques like Fourier transforms or wavelet analysis. While these approaches have their merits, they fall short when dealing with the complex, non-stationary nature of brain signals. Moreover, they tend to overlook contextual information and relationships between different data streams.

In a real-world setting, this limitation translates into suboptimal performance in clinical applications. For instance, analyzing EEG signals for seizure prediction or identifying biomarkers for neurodegenerative diseases requires signal processing techniques that can handle the unique characteristics of brain activity.
