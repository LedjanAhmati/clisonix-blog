The explosion of wearable devices and mobile health sensors has created a deluge of biomedical signals, from electrocardiograms (ECGs) to EEGs. These rich datasets hold the key to unlocking new insights into human physiology, but extracting meaningful information is a daunting task for medical professionals.

**The Problem**

Signal processing in healthcare diagnostics poses significant challenges:

1. **Noise and variability**: Signals are often contaminated with noise from various sources, including instrumentation errors, environmental factors, and individual differences.
2. **Dimensionality and complexity**: Signals have multiple frequency components, time-domain features, and spatial characteristics, making it difficult to identify relevant patterns.
3. **Computational power and scalability**: Processing large datasets in real-time requires significant computational resources, which are often unavailable in resource-constrained medical settings.

**Technical Deep Dive**

Our solution relies on the convergence of three key technologies:

1. **Tide Engine**: A distributed architecture that ensures consistent state across multiple healthcare nodes, enabling seamless collaboration between clinicians and AI systems.
2. **Signal Fabric**: A framework for integrating diverse signal streams from EEGs, audio recordings, biosensors, and other sources, providing a unified view of patient data.

At the heart of our solution lies a hybrid AI approach:

1. **Wavelet denoising**: We apply wavelet-based noise reduction to remove artifacts and extract relevant features.
2. **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks**: LSTMs learn spatial-temporal patterns in signals, enabling predictive modeling of physiological responses.
3. **Graph convolutional networks (GCNs)**: GCNs capture local graph structures, facilitating the analysis of complex networks within biological systems.
