In recent years, there has been an increasing focus on using wearable devices and mobile applications to monitor mental health. However, the raw data generated by these devices is often noisy and requires sophisticated signal processing techniques to extract meaningful insights. One critical aspect of this process is real-time signal filtering.

**The Problem**

Mental health monitoring involves collecting and analyzing various physiological signals from wearables, such as electroencephalography (EEG), electrocardiography (ECG), and skin conductance. However, these signals are subject to significant noise sources, including environmental factors like electromagnetic interference and motion artifacts from the wearer's movements. Traditional filtering techniques can be computationally intensive and may introduce latency, making them unsuitable for real-time applications.

**Technical Deep Dive**

At Clisonix, we address this challenge by leveraging our proprietary technologies: **Tide Engine** and **Signal Fabric**. The Tide Engine ensures consistent state across distributed healthcare nodes, while Signal Fabric weaves together EEG, audio, and biosensor streams to provide a comprehensive view of the wearer's mental state.

Our real-time signal filtering approach is based on a combination of wavelet transform and independent component analysis (ICA). This allows us to separate the underlying signals from noise and artifacts, effectively reducing latency and computational complexity. Specifically:

1.  Wavelet transform decomposes the signal into its constituent frequency bands, enabling selective filtering.
2.  ICA extracts statistically independent components from the signal, isolating relevant features.
