**The Problem**

Signal processing in EEG is a notoriously difficult task due to several reasons:

1. **Noise and artifacts**: EEG signals are susceptible to various forms of noise, such as muscle activity, eye movement, and electrical interference from external sources.
2. **Variability across individuals**: Each person's brain activity exhibits unique patterns, making it challenging to establish a common framework for analysis.
3. **High-dimensional data**: EEG signals consist of multiple channels (typically 32 or more), resulting in an enormous amount of data that requires efficient processing techniques.

**Technical Deep Dive**

These technologies ensure consistent state management across distributed nodes and weave together diverse streams from EEG, audio, and biosensors.

We employ advanced algorithms such as:

1. **Wavelet denoising**: This technique effectively removes noise and artifacts while preserving crucial information within the signal.
2. **Independent component analysis (ICA)**: ICA decomposes mixed signals into their underlying sources, allowing for more accurate identification of brain activity patterns.
3. **Deep learning frameworks**: Our implementation of deep neural networks is specifically designed to handle high-dimensional EEG data, enabling accurate classification and prediction.
