As neurotechnology continues to push the boundaries of human knowledge, the need for advanced signal processing capabilities has never been more pressing. With the increasing availability of electroencephalography (EEG) data, audio recordings, and biosensor streams, researchers are faced with a daunting challenge: extracting meaningful insights from these complex datasets.

**The Problem**

Signal processing in neurotech is plagued by noise, artifacts, and dimensionality issues, making it a significant bottleneck in the development of brain-computer interfaces, neural prosthetics, and cognitive enhancement technologies. Current approaches often rely on hand-engineered features or traditional machine learning algorithms, which are insufficient for tackling the scale and complexity of modern datasets.

Moreover, the lack of standardization in signal processing methodologies has led to inconsistencies across studies, hindering comparability and replication. It is not uncommon for researchers to spend more time debugging their code than actually analyzing data, further exacerbating the problem.
